Many people set goals with the best of intentions, only to abandon them after a while. The first few days, perhaps even weeks, are full of motivation. Then, day by day, that motivation fades, until one day the goal is quietly left behind.

In my experience, the main reason is **comparison**. I have gone through this myself, and I still catch myself doing it.

### The social media trap

Much of this comes from how much time we spend online. We are so absorbed in social media that we lose sight of what we are actually working toward. A 16-year-old posts a video of the five-million-dollar Lamborghini he just bought, and people of the same age, and even those well into their twenties, start measuring their lives against it. The result is predictable: they feel behind, discouraged and unmotivated.

### Why the comparison is unfair

Comparing ourselves with others is rarely a fair contest, for several reasons:

1. **Location.** Opportunities for someone living in the United States are very different from those for someone in Uzbekistan.
2. **Family circumstances.** A person raised in a wealthy family can do far more with their resources than someone from a middle-class background.
3. **Starting point and environment.** Some people started earlier, whether because of their surroundings or the people around them, so they reach success sooner.

We see only the result, never the head start behind it.

### The only comparison that matters

We should stop comparing ourselves with others and start comparing ourselves with **ourselves**.

Every day, every week and every month, it is worth looking back and asking a few honest questions:

- Did I achieve something I could not do before?
- Do I know more than I did last month?
- Am I closer to my goal than I was when I started?

If the answer is yes, even by a small margin, that is real progress. That kind of progress, repeated consistently, is what eventually turns goals into results.
